Redesigning the Poker Game Table

Making a high-density gaming interface easier to scan, understand, and act on. The poker table is where every decision happens — I redesigned Junglee Poker's game table to reduce visual clutter, strengthen information hierarchy, and make critical actions easier to understand, especially for players managing multiple tables.

ProductJunglee Poker
RoleSenior Product Designer
FocusInteraction & visual design

The challenge

The table had to show a lot — without making players think about the interface. Players need to understand their cards, the pot, bets, chip stacks, player states, and available actions, often within seconds.

The existing table was functional, but information competed for attention. Player details were difficult to scan, actions lacked a consistent visual hierarchy, and important gameplay elements could get lost in the interface — particularly noticeable for multi-tabling players, who need to process several tables quickly.

The goal: create a table that feels clear, fast, and immersive without removing the information experienced players rely on.

Five things players need to understand at a glance

Through surveys, in-game feedback, and stakeholder input, we identified the elements that demanded the most attention during gameplay.

  • 01 — Cards. The most important information for decision-making. Players need immediate clarity on their own hand and the shared cards.
  • 02 — Pot value. The total pot helps players understand the risk and reward of each hand.
  • 03 — Bets. Players need to quickly read their own and opponents' bet amounts to understand the flow of action.
  • 04 — Table balance. Chip stacks need to remain visible so players can assess the state of the table, particularly during multi-table play.
  • 05 — Player identity. Names need to be easy to recognise so players can quickly identify opponents during fast-paced games.
The design challenge wasn't to show less information. It was to make the right information easier to find

We looked at how leading poker products solved the same problem

We benchmarked Junglee Poker against Natural8 and PokerBaazi across visual design, information hierarchy, layout and ergonomics, interactivity, feature availability, and performance. The comparison highlighted a clear opportunity: competitors presented gameplay information with stronger hierarchy and richer feedback, while Junglee's table felt more visually crowded and player information was harder to parse.

  • Clearer hierarchy. Important information should immediately stand out.
  • Stronger grouping. Related information should feel like one unit.
  • Better feedback. Player states and game events should be visible without adding noise.

How might we reduce visual noise without reducing the information players need?

From the research and competitive analysis, I focused the redesign around five goals.

Simplify

Reduce unnecessary visual elements and competing information.

Prioritise

Make cards, pot, bets, player state, and actions immediately scannable.

Clarify

Create stronger relationships between related information.

Improve interaction

Make Fold, Call, and Raise easier and faster to use.

Elevate the experience

Create a modern, premium visual language that feels appropriate for a real-money gaming product.

We explored how much information the table really needed to show

The redesign wasn't about simply making everything bigger or removing information — the focus was on grouping, hierarchy, positioning, and interaction.

Every visual change had a reason behind it

Group information players read together

Player name, chip stack, and avatar were grouped into a single visual unit — related information should be processed as one piece rather than several independent elements.

Principle: Gestalt grouping

Put critical actions within easy reach

Fold, Call, and Raise were enlarged and positioned at the bottom of the interface — the most frequent actions should require minimal effort to locate and activate.

Principle: Fitts's Law

Reduce decision friction

Betting was simplified into preset sizes alongside a clear slider — too many choices can slow decision-making, so players should be able to select a bet quickly.

Principle: Hick's Law

Make player states impossible to miss

All-in, Check, Disconnected, and other states received clearer visual treatment — players need to understand what is happening at the table without searching for status information.

Create a stronger visual hierarchy

Cards, pot, bets, player states, and actions received distinct levels of visual emphasis — not everything on a poker table has equal importance at every moment.

A table designed around how players actually scan the game

The redesigned table creates a clearer hierarchy between information, state, and action. Instead of treating every element as equally important, the interface guides attention toward the information players need at each moment.

The result is a cleaner and more focused table that lets players spend less attention understanding the interface and more attention on the game itself.

  • Less visual noise. Unnecessary labels, inconsistent spacing, and competing elements were reduced.
  • Stronger hierarchy. Critical gameplay information is easier to identify and scan.
  • Clearer actions. Fold, Call, and Raise have a consistent and predictable interaction pattern.
  • Better feedback. Player states and game events are more visible.
  • More cohesive visual language. The table moves toward a modern, premium gaming aesthetic.

Designed to disappear behind the game

The final table brings together the new information hierarchy, interaction patterns, player states, and visual system across different gameplay situations.

The goal wasn't to make the table look simpler. It was to make playing feel simpler.

Clarity comes from hierarchy, not reduction

Reflection

A poker table will always be information-dense. Trying to remove everything would only make the experience less useful for experienced players. The better approach was to understand what deserves attention, when it deserves attention, and how quickly players need to find it. That became the foundation for the redesign.
